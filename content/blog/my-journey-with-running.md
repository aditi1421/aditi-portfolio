I did my first 10k when I was in 12th grade. I remember using Runkeeper at that time, which tells you how long ago it was. Now I use Strava like everyone else. I continued to run after, but it was occasional.

I have been running regularly since 2022, when I moved to the US and joined the cross country team at Illinois Tech in the Fall. I enjoyed it, and it was stressful at the same time. Being away from home, juggling classes while also showing up at 5 am everyday for practice.

Cross country is a fun sport. You run along different terrains; hills, grass, mud and it's nothing like running a marathon on the road. In the beginning I was intimidated by other runners. I had been running at my own comfortable pace for years and had lost fitness on top of that, so getting my speed back took longer than I wanted it to. I gave up more than once in my head, decided this wasn't for me. I am grateful to my coaches who always motivated me to keep pushing and provided me the best training. We had track & field in Spring 2023, and I continued to be a part of the team. I remember running the 800m indoors during that time.

I left the team after my Junior year. I still continued to run in Chicago, and participating in multiple runs like the hot chocolate run, shamrock shuffle and many more.

I am in Bangalore now, and Cubbon Park has become my best friend. I love to run here, and I have educated myself more about how to run better, how to practice zone 2 running. Pacing myself & being better. A few months ago I ran 18km in one go, which I want to sit with for a second, because there's a version of me from three years ago who was quitting mid-practice and convinced she didn't belong on that team.

Somewhere along the way, running stopped looking like an exercise and became a part of my life. I rely on it when I am stressed, when life is giving me melons instead of lemons, going through a break up, tackling hard decisions or coming up with ideas to build on claude.

It has been my medium to listen to podcasts, and listen to all kinds of music. Slow jazz music for Zone 2. EDM mix for doing tempo runs. When I don't feel like running, listening to positive music immediately pushes me out of the door.

Running is like second nature now. When I haven't run in a while, I can feel it through fatigue and low levels of concentration.

People tell me they find running boring. I have never understood this. I do this for myself. Most days I am not even competing for a specific time or a target. I am just out there for the love I have towards running.

I am the happiest when I am running outside, when my face has gone completely red. That's how I know it was a good run.

Side note: Food tastes amazing after running :)
